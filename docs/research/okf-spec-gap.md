## What OKF v0.2 actually requires

All citations are to SPEC.md sections at the SHA above.

### Conformance (§11) — the entire hard surface

A bundle is conformant iff:

1. Every non-reserved `.md` file in the tree has a parseable YAML
   frontmatter block.
2. Every frontmatter block has a non-empty `type`.
3. Reserved filenames `index.md` and `log.md`, **when present**, follow §8
   and §9 respectively.

Everything else is SHOULD-level. Consumers **MUST NOT reject** a bundle for:
missing optional fields, unknown `type` values, unknown additional
frontmatter keys, broken cross-links, or missing `index.md` files.

### Required vs optional frontmatter (§4.1)

- **Required: `type` only.** "A concept carrying just `type` is fully
  conformant." A short free string; values are **not registered
  centrally**; consumers must treat unknown types as generic concepts.
- **Recommended:** `title`, `description` (one-line summary), `resource`
  (canonical URI of the described asset), `tags` (list of strings).
- **Optional families:** provenance `sources` (+ sibling `usage_window`),
  trust `generated: {by, at}` / `verified: [{by, at}, ...]` (bare mapping =
  one-element list), lifecycle `status` (`draft | stable | deprecated`,
  absent ⇒ `stable`) and `stale_after: YYYY-MM-DD` (§5); computation fields
  `runtime`, `parameters`, `computation`, `executor`, `attester` for
  `type: Attested Computation` (§10).
- Actor convention (§7): `<producer>/<version>` for agents,
  `human:<id>` for people, `process:<id>` for processes. Trust tiers
  (§5.3) are derived from `verified` and key off the `human:` prefix.

### Unknown extra fields (§4.1 "Extensions")

> "Producers MAY include any additional keys. Consumers SHOULD preserve
> unknown keys when round-tripping and MUST NOT reject documents with
> unrecognized fields."

So Koinome's `prov:*`, `odrl:*`, `summary`, `domain`, `created`,
`updated`, `supersedes`, `synthesis`, `review`, and a per-record `id` (if
added later) are all tolerated by any conformant OKF consumer. Tolerated
is not understood: OKF-native consumers derive provenance/trust only from
`sources` / `generated` / `verified`.

### `index.md` conventions (§3.1, §8, §12)

- `index.md` and `log.md` are **reserved filenames** at every level and
  MUST NOT be used for concept documents.
- Index files contain **no frontmatter**, with one exception: a
  bundle-root `index.md` MAY carry `okf_version: "0.2"` (§12) — the only
  place frontmatter is permitted in an index.
- Body structure: one or more `# Heading` sections, each a bullet list of
  `* [Title](relative-url) - short description` entries; entries SHOULD
  reuse the linked concept's `description`. May be generated by producers
  or synthesized on the fly by consumers; missing indexes are fine.
- `log.md` (§9): date-grouped entries, newest first, ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`
  headings; leading bold words (`**Update**`, ...) are convention only.

### Link resolution (§6)

- Standard markdown links only. Two forms: **bundle-relative absolute**
  starting with `/` (recommended — stable under moves), and ordinary
  **relative** paths.
- Links are untyped directed edges; relationship kind lives in prose.
- **Broken links are not malformed** — consumers must tolerate them
  ("not-yet-written knowledge").
- Path-valued fields (`resource`, `sources[].resource`, `computation`,
  `executor.resource`, `attester.resource`) accept absolute URLs,
  `/`-prefixed bundle-relative paths, or relative paths (§6.2).
- `references/` is a naming convention for mirrored external material,
  not a requirement (§6.3).

### Directory layout (§3)

Free-form: "The directory structure is independent of the domain;
producers organize concepts however makes sense." Concept ID = file path
within the bundle minus `.md` (§2). A bundle may be a repo, a tarball, or
a subdirectory of a larger repo. Koinome's numbered `00-inbox` ...
`99-archive` layout is therefore unconstrained by OKF.

### `type` semantics — collision or fit?

**Fit.** OKF's example values (`BigQuery Table`, `Playbook`, `Metric`)
are illustrative; §4.1 explicitly refuses a central registry and §1
lists "defining a fixed taxonomy of concept types" as a non-goal.
Koinome's `type: decision`, `adr`, `source`, `concept`, `entity`, `moc`
etc. are valid OKF types that a consumer routes as generic concepts.
The only reserved type-adjacent behavior is `type: Attested Computation`
(§10), which Koinome does not use — no collision.

## Gap list (Koinome template/schemas/examples vs OKF v0.2)

Framing: a Koinome corpus is **already consumable** by a conformant OKF
consumer today (the MUST-NOT-reject rules guarantee it). The gaps below
are about (a) the corpus qualifying as a conformant OKF **bundle**, and
(b) Koinome's governance semantics being **legible** to OKF-native
consumers rather than merely tolerated.

### G1 — Non-record `.md` files lack frontmatter — **needs-a-mapping-decision**

`koinome/template/AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` (content: `@AGENTS.md`),
`GEMINI.md`, `README.md`, and all six `docs/*.md` files carry no
frontmatter; same in every example corpus (plus `.koinome/snapshot/`
copies). OKF §11 rules 1–2 require frontmatter with `type` on **every**
non-reserved `.md` in the tree, so the corpus root as-is is a
non-conformant bundle. Two resolutions: (a) add minimal frontmatter
(`type: Reference` or similar) to every operational doc — mechanically
easy, but frontmatter ahead of `@AGENTS.md` in CLAUDE.md needs a smoke
test against agent-harness import behavior; or (b) decide the OKF bundle
is a **projection/export** of the corpus (e.g. `koinome transform`)
rather than the working tree itself, which dissolves G1 entirely. The
decision is really "is the corpus the bundle, or does it export one?" —
that choice also settles G2 and G4.

### G2 — `status` vocabulary collision — **potential wall**

Same key, disjoint value spaces. Koinome `record-common.schema.json`
enum: `active | in-review | done | superseded | archived | accepted |
proposed`. OKF §5.4: `draft | stable | deprecated`, absent ⇒ `stable`.
An OKF consumer reading `status: superseded` faces a value the spec
gives no fallback for (§11's tolerance covers unknown *keys*, not
unknown *values* of a spec-defined key) — it will not reject, but it may
silently misread lifecycle. This is the one gap pure addition cannot
fix: in-place, Koinome would have to either surrender its richer enum or
accept semantic illegibility. Via an export step it reduces to a lossy
but clean mapping (`proposed`/`in-review` → `draft`; `active`/`done`/
`accepted` → `stable`; `superseded`/`archived` → `deprecated`), with the
Koinome value preserved in an extension key (e.g. `koinome:status`).

### G3 — `sources` shape collision — **needs-a-mapping-decision**

Koinome's `concept.md`/`entity.md` templates use
`sources: [05-sources/x.md, ...]` — a list of **strings**. OKF §5.1
defines `sources` as a list of **mappings** with `resource` REQUIRED per
entry (plus optional `id`, `title`, and credibility signals `author`,
`usage_count`, `last_modified`). Same key, incompatible shape: an OKF
consumer finds no `resource` and the provenance edge is lost. Mechanical
rewrite is trivial (`- resource: 05-sources/x.md`), but the decision is
whether Koinome adopts OKF's entry shape natively (gaining per-claim
footnote attribution via `sources[].id`, §5.1) or maps at export time.

### G4 — `60-decisions/index.md` violates the reserved-filename contract — **trivial**

The template's decision-log index carries full Koinome frontmatter
(`type: adr`) and a GFM table body. OKF §3.1/§8: `index.md` is reserved,
must not be a concept document, must contain no frontmatter (non-root),
and uses heading + bullet-list structure — so §11 rule 3 fails. Fix:
rename to e.g. `decision-log.md` (keeps it a first-class record;
reserved name freed), optionally emitting an §8-shaped `index.md`
alongside. Note `koinome/template/70-meetings/`, `80-moc/` etc. have no
`index.md` at all, which is fine — missing indexes are explicitly
tolerated.

### G5 — `summary` vs `description` — **trivial**

Koinome requires `summary`; OKF recommends (not requires) `description`
for index generators and search snippets. Dual-write `description` (or
copy at export). No conflict, only invisibility of summaries to OKF
tooling until mapped.

### G6 — No bundle-root `index.md` / `okf_version` declaration — **trivial**

Optional in OKF, but a root `index.md` with `okf_version: "0.2"` (§12)
plus §8 sections is cheap and is OKF's native progressive-disclosure
entry point. Koinome's `gen_mocs.py` already generates MOCs; the same
machinery can emit §8-shaped indexes.

### G7 — `prov:*` / `generated_at` vs OKF trust family — **needs-a-mapping-decision**

Koinome's `prov:wasAttributedTo` / `prov:wasGeneratedBy` /
`prov:generatedAtTime` ≈ OKF `generated: {by, at}`; `prov:wasDerivedFrom`
≈ `sources[].resource`; the concept template's `generated_at` ≈
`generated.at`. All tolerated as extension keys, but OKF-native
consumers derive provenance and trust tiers **only** from `sources` /
`generated` / `verified` with the §7 actor convention (`human:` prefix
gates the human-reviewed tier). Decision: dual-write the OKF family, or
map at export. Koinome's `review: needed` workflow has a natural target
in OKF `verified` (absence ⇒ unverified tier) — adopting it would make
Koinome's review state legible as OKF trust tiers for free.

### G8 — `odrl:*` boundary policies and `koinome.corpus.yaml` — **trivial** (no counterpart, no conflict)

OKF has no policy/governance layer (§1 non-goals) and no manifest file;
`koinome.corpus.yaml` is a non-`.md` file, which OKF ignores, and
`odrl:*` keys ride along as extensions. Nothing collides — but nothing
carries either: OKF consumers will not enforce ODRL boundary policies.
That is out of OKF's scope by design, not a mappable gap.

### G9 — `type` values — **trivial** (fit, see analysis above)

`type: decision` / `adr` / `source` / `moc` are conformant. Only note:
Koinome uses both `decision` (template) and `adr` (scaffolded records) —
worth unifying for OKF consumers that filter by type, but OKF itself
doesn't care.

### G10 — per-record `id` — **trivial**

Contrary to the ticket's premise, no `id` frontmatter key currently
appears in `koinome/template/`, `koinome/schemas/v0/record-common.schema.json`,
or the examples (identity is corpus-level `corpus_id` in
`koinome.corpus.yaml`). If introduced, it is a tolerated extension key.
Mild caveat: OKF gives `sources[].id` a specific meaning (footnote join
key, §5.1) — a top-level `id` does not collide with that.

### G11 — links — **trivial**

Koinome records use plain relative markdown links
(e.g. `[ADR-0002](adr-0002-service-boundaries.md)`), one of OKF's two
supported forms. OKF merely *recommends* `/`-prefixed bundle-relative
links; broken links are tolerated. ADR-0001 already chose open-standard
links over wikilinks, which is exactly OKF's model.

### G12 — MOCs and `71-log/` — **trivial**

MOC files and dated log records are ordinary concepts under OKF (no
`log.md` file exists, so §9 is not triggered). OKF consumers won't use
MOCs for progressive disclosure (they look for `index.md`), but nothing
breaks.

## Bottom line

OKF v0.2's hard surface is tiny (frontmatter + non-empty `type`
everywhere; reserved `index.md`/`log.md` shape) and its tolerance rules
mean Koinome corpora are already safely *consumable*. Full *producer*
conformance in-place needs: frontmatter on operational docs (G1), a
decision-log rename (G4), and a `sources` reshape (G3) — all cheap. The
single structural sore spot is the `status` key collision (G2), which is
unsolvable by addition alone and pushes toward the cleaner architecture:
treat OKF as an **export target** (`koinome transform` emitting a
conformant bundle with the G2/G3/G5/G7 mappings applied) rather than
making the working corpus double as the bundle. No gap found rises to a
hard wall under the export model.
